## COGITATE: the cross-theory comparison (IIT and GNWT)

The Consilience consciousness-science demo ([consilience-demo.netlify.app](https://consilience-demo.netlify.app)) compares theories of consciousness claim by claim. Its results for IIT against GNWT and IIT against Orch-OR are pinned, with their SHA-256 hashes, in the public engine repository at commit `96ba4e435bd13718d36435cc3436d938d2d1f894`: [results/demo/](https://github.com/Anuja-Khatri/theconsilience-engine/blob/96ba4e435bd13718d36435cc3436d938d2d1f894/results/demo/README.md). Every record below links to exact lines of those files and carries its content hash.

**The COGITATE blind test.** The demo was checked blind against the preregistered predictions of the COGITATE adversarial collaboration (IIT against GNWT): **2 of 3 after a disclosed re-review, originally 1 of 3, p = 0.075** against a 1,000-key permutation null. That is suggestive only. Method and full numbers: [10.5281/zenodo.23672883](https://zenodo.org/records/23672883). There is no record for this test: none of the templates fits a recovery score, so it is reported here and in the receipt README, not as a nanopublication.

**What is published.** The demo finds three distinct IIT-against-GNWT disagreements that it rules testable with instruments that exist today: where consciousness is located, its time course, and connectivity. They involve eight claim pairs. The testable-disagreement template takes exactly one claim A and one claim B, so there is one record per claim pair (eight), grouped below under the three disagreements, plus one claim card for each of the eleven claims involved.

**How to read the claim statements.** Each claim statement is a paraphrase of the theory paper, drafted by a language model and reviewed by a person. **It is not a quotation.** Each claim card gives the source document and the page(s) it rests on; a page is the page of the PDF the demo read, which can differ from the printed page number. Every record says this in its publication info, and every claim card states `cv:statementForm cv:ReviewedParaphrase`. A testable disagreement is a statement about two claim cards, not a verdict on either theory.
