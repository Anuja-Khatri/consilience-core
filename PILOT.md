## Changes from v1 (after review)

Tobias Kuhn's review (18 Nov 2026) found v1 technically clean (valid signatures, records matching their templates) and asked for four design changes before going live.

1. **Give claims their own identity.** v1 identified a claim by a GitHub line range, so a corrected card at a new commit would have orphaned every refusal pointing at it. Now each claim-card nanopub *introduces* its own claim IRI (`npx:introduces <…/claim>`), refusals and disagreements name claim A and claim B by those IRIs, and the GitHub receipt (pinned commit, line range, content hash) is in the provenance only. A corrected card supersedes the old one (`npx:supersedes`) and introduces the *same* claim IRI, so the links stay intact: see [Correcting a claim card](#correcting-a-claim-card-supersession). Every claim a refusal names now has a published claim card, so eight supporting claim cards (records 11 to 18) were added to the ten v1 ledger records. Tests: [section 1 of `test_review_v2.py`](https://github.com/Anuja-Khatri/consilience-core/blob/nanopub-v2/tests/test_review_v2.py).
2. **People in the provenance.** The agent introduction names Anuja Khatri's ORCID iD as `frbr:owner` (a new introduction, so the agent IRI changed). The templates are attributed to her ORCID iD and signed by the engine's key; on the live network, trust comes from Tobias Kuhn approving the engine's introduction, as he offered and the owner accepted. Each theory claim card names its reviewer (`cv:reviewedBy` her ORCID iD) and says it was drafted by a large language model (`cv:draftedBy "a large language model"`). By the owner's decision no model name or version is recorded. Tests: `test_published_template_matches_its_spec_and_is_attributed_to_the_owner`, `test_introduction_names_the_owner_by_orcid` ([test_trig.py](https://github.com/Anuja-Khatri/consilience-core/blob/nanopub-v2/tests/test_trig.py)), `test_signed_trig_matches_the_envelope` ([test_theories.py](https://github.com/Anuja-Khatri/consilience-core/blob/nanopub-v2/tests/test_theories.py)).
3. **IRIs over strings.** Conditions, axes, units, techniques, theories, dimensions, positions and observables are IRIs. Existing vocabularies are reused where they fit (QUDT units and quantity kinds, ChEBI for the electrolyte, CHMO for chronopotentiometry, Wikidata for IIT); the rest are minted under `cv:` with labels, comments and SKOS mappings ([docs/VOCABULARY.md](docs/VOCABULARY.md)). Conditions are structured triples (condition IRI, number, unit IRI; or condition IRI and value IRI), not JSON strings. Refusals say which claim lacks a condition (record 04: only claim B lacks the electrolyte) and record a condition that differs with its IRI and both values (record 04: current density, 10 vs 100 mA/cm², rule CR2); the rule id is kept as a separate field. Tests: [section 3 of `test_review_v2.py`](https://github.com/Anuja-Khatri/consilience-core/blob/nanopub-v2/tests/test_review_v2.py).
4. **Template (c).** The "testable disagreement" template is replaced by a general disagreement template: claim A, claim B, the dimension of disagreement (an IRI), the observables and the instruments as separate fields, and a testability status from {testable now, testable under a stated assumption, not yet testable}, always with the reason. "Not testable" is no longer a value anywhere, and observables (such as regional decoding) are no longer filed as instruments; the theory claim template uses "dimension" and "position" instead of "quantity axis". One template serves both measured and theory disagreements (the margin, floor and unit are an optional group), so no separate theory variant was needed. Tests: [section 4 of `test_review_v2.py`](https://github.com/Anuja-Khatri/consilience-core/blob/nanopub-v2/tests/test_review_v2.py).

## Correcting a claim card (supersession)

Nanopublications cannot be edited or deleted. If a claim card has to be corrected (for example, the ledger fixes the card at a new commit), the engine publishes a new claim-card nanopub that

- states `npx:supersedes <old nanopub>` in its publication info (and names the Knowledge Pixels "Supersedes previous version" pubinfo template);
- introduces the **same** claim IRI, written out in full rather than as `sub:claim`, in the assertion and in `npx:introduces`;
- carries the new receipt (new commit, line range, content hash) in its provenance.

Refusals and disagreements keep pointing at the claim IRI, so none of them needs republishing; readers follow the supersedes chain to the current card. It has to be signed with the same key as the old version, on the same network.

A **dry run** (not published) is in [`docs/examples/supersede-claim-card-01.dry-run.trig`](docs/examples/supersede-claim-card-01.dry-run.trig): it supersedes claim card 01 ([`RA-wwFLLedct…`](https://test.registry.knowledgepixels.com/np/RA-wwFLLedctNlBcSNtQWif97OzFredyblCEDRMo6TmRU)) and introduces `https://w3id.org/np/RA-wwFLLedctNlBcSNtQWif97OzFredyblCEDRMo6TmRU/claim`, the IRI that refusals 04 and 06 point at. Signed locally, it passed the nanopub-skill `supersede_check.py` ("keeps every IRI that the superseded version introduces") and `check-nanopub-conformance.py`. Command:

```bash
consilience export --nanopub pilot/records/01-claim-card-ref40_peng2023__002.json --supersedes https://w3id.org/np/RA-wwFLLedctNlBcSNtQWif97OzFredyblCEDRMo6TmRU --dry-run
```

## COGITATE: the cross-theory comparison (IIT and GNWT)

The Consilience consciousness-science demo ([consilience-demo.netlify.app](https://consilience-demo.netlify.app)) compares theories of consciousness claim by claim. Its results for IIT against GNWT and IIT against Orch-OR are pinned, with their SHA-256 hashes, in the public engine repository at commit `96ba4e435bd13718d36435cc3436d938d2d1f894`: [results/demo/](https://github.com/Anuja-Khatri/theconsilience-engine/blob/96ba4e435bd13718d36435cc3436d938d2d1f894/results/demo/README.md). Every record below links to exact lines of those files in its provenance and carries its content hash.

**The COGITATE blind test.** The demo was checked blind against the preregistered predictions of the COGITATE adversarial collaboration (IIT against GNWT): **2 of 3 after a disclosed re-review, originally 1 of 3, p = 0.075** against a 1,000-key permutation null. That is suggestive only. Method and full numbers: [10.5281/zenodo.23672883](https://zenodo.org/records/23672883). There is no record for this test: none of the templates fits a recovery score, so it is reported here and in the receipt README, not as a nanopublication.

**What is published.** The demo finds three distinct IIT-against-GNWT disagreements that it rules testable now: where consciousness is located, its time course, and connectivity. They involve eight claim pairs. The disagreement template takes exactly one claim A and one claim B, so there is one record per claim pair (eight), plus one claim card for each of the eleven claims involved. The demo names the observables each disagreement bears on; it names no instrument, so no instrument is recorded.

**How to read the claim statements.** Each claim statement is a paraphrase of the theory paper, drafted by a large language model and reviewed by Anuja Khatri (ORCID [0009-0008-3375-7858](https://orcid.org/0009-0008-3375-7858)), who is named in each card's provenance. **It is not a quotation.** Each claim card gives the source document and the page(s) it rests on; a page is the page of the PDF the demo read, which can differ from the printed page number. Every claim card states `cv:statementForm cv:ReviewedParaphrase`. A disagreement is a statement about two claims, not a verdict on either theory.
